What is the AI Act? <!--anki:4a5b34484f4550646148-->
?
The **Regulation (EU) 2024/1689**, entered into force on **1 August 2024**, frames AI with progressive application. Obligations depend in particular on the use, the role of the actor and the category of the system or model.

Example: Providing a general model and deploying a recruitment system do not correspond to the same role or obligations. A non-EU company may be concerned when supplying the European market or when exits are used in the EU.

Check **application field, exclusions, qualification and calendar** in up-to-date texts; neither hosting outside the EU nor open license is sufficient to conclude an exemption.

---

What are the risk levels of the AI Act? <!--anki:722d2145524261493c49-->
?
1. **Unacceptable**: practices **forbidden**.
2. **High risk**: heavy obligations (risk management, data, documentation, human supervision, compliance).
3. **Transparency risk** Bonds ’**public information** (Chatbots, generated content, deepfakes).
4. **Minimum risk**: no high risk regime; check cross-cutting obligations and other applicable texts.

In addition, there is a regime specific to **general-purpose AI models** (GPAI).

---

What AI practices does the AI Act prohibit? <!--anki:6e6f4a2c7d6c3c7c4856-->
?
In particular: the **Manipulation** subliminal or exploitation of the vulnerabilities causing harm, the **social notation**, the **Recognition of emotions** The **Work** and the **School** (except for medical or safety reasons), on the **Non-targeted harvesting** of facial images to build bases of recognition, Biometric categorization on **Sensitive features**, and, With strict exceptions, the **Real-time remote biometric identification** in public space by law enforcement. Prohibitions Applicable Since **2 February 2025**.

What is a high-risk system? <!--anki:633821384664284a3071-->
?
Two main ways to article 6:

- **Appendix I products**: AI is an affected safety product or component and the product must undergo a third-party conformity assessment.
- **Appendix III uses** include certain employment applications, education, biometrics, credit, justice or public services.

The purpose and the actual influence on the decision count; the article 6 provides conditions and exceptions to be examined, with a special regime for profiling. A ranking tool is a characteristic example. The mere presence of AI in a sector is not enough to qualify all its uses.

---

What are the requirements for a high-risk system? <!--anki:754c3a4e344f493f6f29-->
?
System of **Risk Management**, **Data Governance** (quality, bias), **Technical Documentation**, **Logging** automatic, **Transparency** to the deployers, **Human supervision** effective, **accuracy, Robustness and Cybersecurity**, quality management system, **conformity assessment** and CE marking, registration in the EU database, **Post-marketing surveillance**.

Distribute obligations according to the applicable role and category. Example: Recruitment supervision requires a competent, knowledgeable, and capable human to challenge the ranking, not just an approval button. Link each requirement to a maintained evidence and check the applicable timeline.

---

Do not confuse: supplier and deployer? <!--anki:6a68263531675a333877-->
?
- **Supplier**: the one who **develops** the system (or causes it to develop) and places it on the market under his name — carries the bulk of the obligations.
- **Deployer** : The one who **uses** the system in its activity — Bonds **compliant use**, of **Human supervision**, information of people, sometimes of **impact assessment on fundamental rights**.

Caution: **Substantially changing** a system or using it for a high-risk purpose can make **become a supplier**.

---

What are the transparency requirements for common LLM applications? <!--anki:4a2a496d2b5769366f51-->
?
The **Article 50** distinguishes roles: information about the interaction with an AI for certain systems, machine detectable marking for some synthetic generation suppliers, and disclosure of certain deepfakes or texts of public information by the deployers.

Exceptions depend on disposition and use; A human re-reading does not universally exempt all content. The general application begins at **2 August 2026**. The article 111(4) grants up to **2 December 2026** to comply with the marking of the article 50(2systems already on the market before the 2 August 2026. This targeted postponement does not suspend all transparency obligations or those of the GDPR.

---

What are the requirements for General Purpose AI (GPAI) models? <!--anki:465e51482b414f48404f-->
?
The **GPAI Suppliers** are concerned by technical documentation, information to integrators, copyright policy and public summary of training data, according to the applicable obligations and exceptions.

The application starts on **2 August 2025**, but the models already on the market before that date have a transition until **2 August 2027** according to the article 111(3).

**Systemic risk**, including presumed beyond 10²⁵ FLOPs training, adds assessments, risk management, serious incident reporting and cybersecurity. Identify role, date and category; the code of best practice helps to demonstrate compliance without replacing the regulation.

Which AI Act application schedule to remember at 6 October 2026? <!--anki:4c30777735412e43217c-->
?
Calendar **Regulated on 13 November 2026**, regulation (EU) 2026/1744 included:
- **2 February 2025**: first prohibitions and AI control.
- **2 August 2025**: governance and GPAI obligations, according to transitions.
- **2 August 2026**: general application, including transparency, subject to conditions.
- **2 December 2026**: new prohibitions specified in 113(a), and end of the 50(2) marking transition for systems prior to 2 August 2026.
- **2 December 2027**: Sections 1 to 3 of Chapter III for Annex III; **2 August 2028** for Annex I, excluding Article 6(5).

Check disposition, role and date of placing on the market.

What are the penalties under the AI Act? <!--anki:714d7b7e6959667d2b6f-->
?
The **articles 99 and 101** distinguish in particular:
- **Prohibited practices**: 35 M€ or 7% annual worldwide sales.
- **Obligations referred to in 99(4)**: 15 M€ or 3%.
- **Incorrect, incomplete or misleading information** to national authorities or notified bodies: 7.5 M€ or 1%.
- **GPAI suppliers**, sanctioned by the Commission according to article 101: 15 M€ or 3%.

For a company, normally retain the highest ceiling; Article 99 provides the lowest for SMEs and certain breaches of SMCs. These are ceilings, not automatic fines: circumstances and proportionality count.

With the AI Act, what does an AI Engineer start with? <!--anki:66347c2e784c3d563250-->
?
1. **Inventory** of AI systems and use cases.
2. **Classify** each (banned, high risk, transparency, minimal) and identify **role**: supplier or deployer.
3. Obtain the **documentation** from GPAI model providers.

Describe the actual purpose, the people involved and the effect of the exit on their rights. The same technology can support a simple drafting or selection of candidates, with different obligations. Document the qualification with the legal and identify the evidence to be produced; the business name of the model does not determine the category.

---

What do we do next, according to the AI Act? <!--anki:6538416e7056465a5358-->
?
1. For simple cases: **transparency** (AI mention, marking of the generated content).
2. For high risk: risk management, **journalization**, **human supervision**, documentation and evaluations **from conception**.
3. Articulate with [[154-gdpr-llm|RGPD]] and standards like ISO 42001 ([[105-agentic-ai-devsecops|Repositories]]).

For a high-risk system, newspapers must be able to trace the operation: model version, input, result and human intervention. Review the classification with each change of use: an internal assistant who begins to evaluate employees is a job, a high-risk area, without a line of code has changed.

---

Why is adding human validation not enough to remove a recruitment system from the high-risk regime of the AI Act? <!--anki:6531323530623364343638393435333738346366373835306438383866336637-->
?
The **classification** depends on the purpose and conditions of Article 6 and Annex III, not just the presence of a human click. A ranking of applications can strongly influence the decision even if a recruiter confirms it.

Describe the actual use, possible exceptions and the role of each actor with the legal. Design effective supervision and retain evidence of qualification. Human control is part of the control measures; it does not constitute a general exemption nor a guarantee that the decision remains independent of the score.

---

Why can a change in purpose require a review of an organization's role under the AI Act? <!--anki:3263366433306435656661383432656561356465663737633534616537343864-->
?
Article 25 provides in particular that an actor modifying **the intended purpose of a system to make it high risk** can be considered as a supplier, under the conditions of the text.

The qualification depends on the system and the modification, not the commercial title of the product. Documenting the initial purpose, new use and responsibilities, and then checking the applicable obligations and dates. Any prompt adjustment is not automatically a substantial modification.

---

Why keep an AI Act qualification file per use case rather than a single label for an entire model? <!--anki:3430366563393030646465323430616638633366336366353061666637336534-->
?
The same model can participate in **different systems and purposes**. The obligations related to the general-purpose model and those of the deployed system are not confused.

Document usage, actors, affected population, degree of autonomy and justification of qualification. Re-examine the file if the perimeter changes. A global "compliant" label does not demonstrate compliance with all the obligations of a new use case.

## Scenarios

Scenario: your company deploys three AI systems: an internal writing assistant, a customer chatbot and a pre-selection tool. How do you classify them? <!--anki:4943714a5b34316b7d70-->
?
1. **Internal writing assistant**: generally off-high risk; check for actual use, AI proficiency and other applicable rules
2. **Chatbot client**: obligation to **transparency**, inform that we are talking to an AI
3. **Pre-selection of applications**: **high risk** (employment, annex III), with risk management, logging, effective human supervision, documentation
4. **Determine role**: Deployer of a purchased system, or provider if you develop or modify it substantially
5. **Check the current calendar**, which has been amended since the original text

**Trap** Sort by technology rather than by **Use** : it is the employment context that makes a system high risk.

---

Scenario: a team wants to use emotion detection on calls from a contact center, to evaluate the advisors. What do you answer? <!--anki:66633f6f37312a535b6a-->
?
1. **Qualify the actual function**: infer the emotions of employees from biometric signals, whose voice, falls under the prohibition at work, unless medical or security exceptions apply.
2. **Suspend this device** and have the qualification confirmed by the legal; a final aggregation does not erase a prohibited inference upstream.
3. **Study another collection**, for example a satisfaction questionnaire, without reintroducing this inference or disproportionate monitoring.
4. **Check the remaining obligations**: purpose, legal basis, information, minimisation and consultation of the competent authorities.
5. **Document the decision** and test that the product does not retain an equivalent function under another name.

**Piece**: Present anonymization or consent as an automatic exemption from a prohibition.
