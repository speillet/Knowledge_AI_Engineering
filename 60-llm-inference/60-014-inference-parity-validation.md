What does an inference parity test check, and how does it differ from a quality eval? <!--anki:3138343630313632333762373438663462306238333434366266376133643264-->
?
It checks that the serving stack computes **the same model** as a reference: same input tokens, same output distributions, up to rounding errors. A **quality eval** measures whether the model handles the task well.

The two complement each other: a faithful server can serve a mediocre model, and an eval can stay stable while a defect only affects long contexts or one language. Run parity at every change of engine, version, kernel, parallelism or option such as prefix caching; evals remain the release gate ([[112-model-cicd|eval gate]]).

---

Why is comparing the text generated by two servers not enough to validate their parity? <!--anki:3339336236623065333732373434363361363565313564366331313763653537-->
?
Because a single **token flip** makes the whole continuation diverge: the text that follows is conditioned on a different prefix. Two greedy outputs that diverge at the 40th token do not prove a bug, and two identical outputs on short prompts do not prove the absence of a defect.

Compare **distributions under a forced prefix** instead, as in [[51-fine-tuning-adaptation|teacher forcing]]: at each position, both stacks receive exactly the same context. Differences then stay local and measurable instead of propagating.

---

Calculation: each token of a greedy output has an independent 0.1% probability of being flipped by numerical noise. Over 256 tokens, what share of outputs diverges from the reference text? <!--anki:3736303239653136356634313461653361643530323063613863626539626235-->
?
```text
P(no flip) = 0.999^256 ≈ e^(−0.256) ≈ 0.774
P(divergence) ≈ 1 − 0.774 ≈ 22.6%
```
Nearly **one output in four** differs from the reference text, without any bug. An "identical text" criterion would fail on long answers, or force you to tolerate differences without knowing which ones are serious. Independence is a simplification: flips concentrate on positions where two tokens are nearly tied.

---

How do you measure the parity of two inference stacks with teacher forcing? <!--anki:3066336638653833656231353439663538306664306135613364653661353361-->
?
Build (prompt, reference continuation) pairs and have each stack **score the continuation**: log-probability of each forced token and top-k alternatives. With vLLM, the `prompt_logprobs` parameter returns these values for the prompt tokens, continuation included:

```python
SamplingParams(max_tokens=1, prompt_logprobs=5)
```

Compare position by position: log-probability gap of the forced token, top-1 agreement, KL divergence over the top-k ([[68-quantization|comparison metrics]]). Continuations must cover the lengths, languages and formats actually served.

---

Why does a parity test based on prompt_logprobs not cover vLLM's prefix caching? <!--anki:3462653032633661386532613438326439643765363334353334303933666562-->
?
Because vLLM **bypasses the prefix cache** for a request that asks for `prompt_logprobs`: it recomputes the whole prefill to produce these values. The path actually used in production, reusing KV cache blocks, is therefore not exercised.

Add generation tests: send prompts with a shared prefix, with the cache enabled then disabled, and compare the logprobs of **generated** tokens or the greedy outputs. A parity test only covers the code paths it goes through ([[66-prefix-caching-radix-attention|prefix caching]]).

---

What closeness criterion does vLLM use to compare its outputs with those of Hugging Face Transformers? <!--anki:3664316261323639623662643462383161663863343631376238303863376533-->
?
Two levels in its model tests: **exact equality** of the text, and **logprob closeness**: each token chosen by vLLM must appear in the top-k logprobs of Transformers, and vice versa.

This criterion tolerates a near-tied token changing rank, but catches a token that the reference considered unlikely. It carries over to any stack change: compare up to the first divergence, then check that each choice remains plausible for the other implementation. The reference must use the same weights, dtype and sampling parameters.

---

Why classify top-1 flips by the reference's margin? <!--anki:3063643362336663333866373462323962323135613930633832336230653465-->
?
A flip between two tokens whose log-probabilities differ by 0.01 is **rounding noise**; a flip where the reference had a 3-nat lead signals a **computation defect**.

Compute at each position the margin between the reference's top-1 and top-2, then count low-margin and high-margin flips separately. The former are tolerated within a measured limit; the latter must be close to zero. A global top-1 agreement rate mixes both and can hide a few clear errors behind a 99% score.

---

Calculation: in BF16, the mantissa keeps 8 significant bits. What is the gap between two representable values around a logit of 20? <!--anki:3738313362353564366537393433386438346264363461376563663864333234-->
?
Numbers in the interval [16, 32) are written 2⁴ × m, with m coded on 8 significant bits:
```text
step = 2^4 × 2^(−7) = 2^(−3) = 0.125
```
Two logits of 20.00 and 20.06 may be rounded to the same value or kept apart depending on the order of operations. This is why nearly tied tokens change rank between two correct kernels. The calculation assumes logits stored in BF16; producing or accumulating logits in FP32 reduces this gap, without removing the rounding of earlier layers.

---

How do you set the tolerance threshold of an inference parity test? <!--anki:3534343564643366303333363462346239616134313766653239626238616161-->
?
First measure the **noise floor**: the reference compared with itself under two legitimate conditions, for example batch size 1 and batch size 32, or two identical GPUs. This gives the normal distribution of logprob gaps and the rate of low-margin flips.

The candidate stack must stay within this floor, with a margin set before the run, per length, language or format segment. A threshold chosen after seeing the results ends up absorbing the defect you are looking for. For debugging, vLLM's **batch-invariant** mode, in beta (`VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1`), removes this source of noise at a performance cost.

---

How do you check that two inference stacks send exactly the same tokens to the model? <!--anki:6233333563616138613130623463313462626435346264373465326133613063-->
?
Compare the **token IDs** of the rendered prompt, not the text. On the reference side, use `apply_chat_template(..., tokenize=True, add_generation_prompt=True)` from Transformers; on the vLLM side, the `/tokenize` endpoint accepts `messages`:

```bash
curl -s localhost:8000/tokenize -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"messages":[{"role":"user","content":"Hello"}],"add_generation_prompt":true}'
```

Inspect the beginning and the end: role markers, answer prefix, single BOS. A difference here often explains a drop wrongly blamed on the weights or the kernels ([[11-llm-inference-servers|chat template]]).

---

Why does a duplicate BOS degrade the answers of a served LLM? <!--anki:3764626164663330353331343439373862653637396162663065313063636162-->
?
The **BOS** marks the start of the sequence. If the chat template already inserts it and the tokenizer adds a second one, the model receives a sequence absent from its training: answers often remain plausible, but quality drops without any visible error.

The trap appears when you tokenize an already rendered prompt yourself with `add_special_tokens=True`. For messages, vLLM's `/tokenize` endpoint sets this parameter to `false` by default, precisely because the template adds the special tokens. Check the first IDs of each test prompt.

---

Why set every sampling parameter explicitly in a parity test? <!--anki:3036656434326661623062623434613862303663323065613333356538613633-->
?
Because **default** values differ from one stack to another. Since v0.8.0, vLLM applies the `generation_config.json` of the model repository when the request specifies nothing: temperature, `top_p` or `top_k` change without any client change. `--generation-config vllm` reverts to the engine's neutral values.

Transformers also reads this file in `generate`. Set temperature, `top_p`, `top_k`, penalties, `max_tokens` and stop sequences in every request, then log the effective configuration. Otherwise, a settings difference passes for a model difference.

---

Why check the logprobs mode before comparing two inference servers? <!--anki:3332313835636130373865373465623739323861363533303236393833363864-->
?
Because a server can return logprobs **before or after** the sampling transformations. vLLM V1 returns the model's **raw** logprobs by default, before temperature, penalties and filters; `--logprobs-mode processed_logprobs` returns the values after processing, temperature and `top_k`/`top_p` included.

Comparing raw logprobs with processed ones creates a false gap, larger as the temperature moves away from 1. Document each stack's mode, or compare at temperature 1 without filters ([[65-probabilities-sampling|probabilities and sampling]]).

---

How do you validate the tool and reasoning parsers of an inference server? <!--anki:3262313839643062353632373461623862316530633531353434653634313539-->
?
By checking **where** each part of the answer lands. With vLLM, tool calls are only extracted if `--enable-auto-tool-choice` and the right `--tool-call-parser` are active. Reasoning is only separated with `--reasoning-parser`, in the `reasoning` field, formerly named `reasoning_content`.

Test both without and with streaming: `tool_calls` filled, arguments as valid JSON, content without leftover tags. A client that still reads `reasoning_content` gets an empty field without any error: the contract must also be tested on the client side ([[32-tool-calling|tool calling]]).

---

Why test structured outputs after changing the engine or the constrained decoding backend? <!--anki:3132323337613961616632383464623262373762653536376562646462313165-->
?
Because each backend implements its own subset of JSON Schema, regex and grammars. In vLLM, the `auto` mode picks the backend per request; LM Format Enforcer follows Python's regex syntax, XGrammar and Guidance follow Rust's. Changing the backend changes the allowed tokens, and therefore the outputs.

Replay each production schema with cases that **must** be constrained: maximum length, enumerations, patterns, required fields. Check that the outputs actually respect these constraints, not just that they parse ([[63-guided-generation|guided generation]]).

---

How do you use lm-evaluation-harness to validate an inference server? <!--anki:3936613165636231396633663430326538356333643430633836613663393138-->
?
As an **end-to-end check** at task level, on the real server:

```bash
lm_eval --model local-completions --tasks gsm8k \
  --model_args model=my-model,base_url=http://localhost:8000/v1/completions,num_concurrent=8 \
  --log_samples --output_path results/
```

Run the same configuration on the reference and compare **example by example** thanks to `--log_samples`. Likelihood-scored tasks, like many multiple-choice tasks, require the completions API: `local-chat-completions` only supports generative tasks. `--apply_chat_template` changes the prompt format; keep it identical on both sides.

---

Why not compare your eval score with the figure published in a model card? <!--anki:6635303565363137323831373464323861663239383530623063356566333031-->
?
Because the published figure depends on choices you do not necessarily reproduce: number of few-shot examples, reasoning, chat template, sampling, answer extraction, task version. A few points of difference are often explained by these choices, not by the server.

The right reference is **your own run** of the same configuration on a trusted stack, for example Transformers or the version in production. You then compare two paired runs ([[114-reproducibility-variance|paired comparison]]) and the examples that change status.

---

How do you check the parity of an inference deployment under load? <!--anki:6333616239636461326238643463363139303063653832316264356234313863-->
?
By replaying the parity set **at the target concurrency**, not only request by request. Load changes the batch composition, triggers preemption and KV cache recomputation, and uses other CUDA graph sizes.

Compare the logprobs obtained at concurrency 1 and under load: gaps at the noise floor are normal ([[114-reproducibility-variance|batch invariance]]); high-margin flips or incoherent text signal a defect. Also force preemption, for example by reducing the KV cache memory, to exercise this rare path.

---

How do you validate an inference deployment across the served context lengths? <!--anki:3263316331643763623539373438636662613662646565633536626261333639-->
?
By testing at real lengths, up to `max_model_len`, not only on short prompts. Context extension (RoPE scaling, YaRN), sliding windows or chunked prefill only come into play beyond certain thresholds.

Place facts to retrieve at several depths, for example 10%, 50% and 90% of the window, and measure logprob parity on the last positions, per length bucket. An ignored or miscopied RoPE configuration leaves short prompts intact and sharply degrades long ones.

---

What does a versioned inference parity set contain? <!--anki:6535303366306164353065643435616161326639663433383531376364306162-->
?
- **Rendered prompts** and their token IDs, to freeze the input.
- **Reference continuations** with the top-k logprobs of the trusted stack.
- Complete sampling **parameters** and the logprobs mode.
- **Segments**: lengths, languages, tools, structured outputs, shared prefixes.
- **Thresholds** derived from the noise floor, per segment.

Version this set with the reference — weights, dtype, engine — and replay it at every update of the engine, driver or image ([[112-model-cicd|model CI/CD]]).

---

How do you decide between two inference stacks that diverge beyond the noise floor? <!--anki:3462633933396566323533373465323338313237613332643235326630376438-->
?
With a **more precise arbiter**: the reference implementation, with the same weights converted to FP32. Converting BF16 to FP32 adds no error; only the computations become less rounded. Being slower, this arbiter is reserved for the divergent positions.

Measure each stack's logprob gap to the arbiter. A stack clearly further away than the other is suspect; two stacks at a comparable distance reflect rounding. Check that the arbiter's FP32 matrix multiplications do not run in **TF32**: PyTorch disables it by default for matmuls, but code can enable it ([[114-reproducibility-variance|non-determinism]]).

---

How do you validate moving an embedding model to another serving engine? <!--anki:3738616635613730336263653431383939303834373836623666643935343633-->
?
Same weights do not mean same vectors: the engine also decides **pooling**, **normalization**, **prefixes** and **truncation**. In vLLM, `--pooler-config` takes precedence over the Sentence Transformers metadata, which takes precedence over the architecture default: missing or wrong metadata changes the pooling without any error.

1. Compare, text by text, the cosine between reference and served vectors, long texts included.
2. Check the vector norm and the prefix added to queries.
3. Measure the top-k overlap and recall@k on annotated queries.

Only query the existing index after this check ([[133-embeddings-representations|embeddings]]).

Calculation: for Qwen2.5-VL, one visual token covers 28 × 28 pixels. A 4,000 × 3,000 pixel photo goes through a stack that applies the repository's preprocessor_config.json (max_pixels = 16,384 × 28 × 28) and through another set to max_pixels = 1,280 × 28 × 28. Roughly how many visual tokens on each side? <!--anki:3536663064656366656332373462373339623835393964323533613531396539-->
?
```text
stack A: 12,000,000 px ≤ 12,845,056 px → no downscaling
         12,000,000 / 784 ≈ 15,300 visual tokens
stack B: image downscaled below 1,003,520 px → at most 1,280 tokens
```
The two stacks do not see the same input: about **12 times** more tokens on one side, hence other readable details, another cost and another latency. Before comparing outputs, align the image processor parameters, which vLLM receives through `mm_processor_kwargs`, and compare `usage.prompt_tokens` for the same image ([[161-vision-language-models|VLM]]).

How does llama.cpp's GGUF conversion avoid splitting text differently from the original model? <!--anki:3866373265316232626138313435343738616663383665666234623964363037-->
?
The **pre-tokenizer**, which splits spaces, digits and punctuation before BPE, is reimplemented in llama.cpp. The converter encodes a tricky text with the original tokenizer: spaces and tabs, numbers, emojis, several scripts, apostrophes. It computes the SHA-256 hash of the resulting IDs. A known hash selects the pre-tokenizer recorded in the GGUF; an unknown one **stops the conversion** ("BPE pre-tokenizer was not recognized").

General lesson: validate a converted tokenizer on difficult strings by comparing the IDs on both sides, for example with the `/tokenize` endpoint of `llama-server`. Never force a hash by hand ([[164-local-edge-llm|local LLM]]).

## Scenarios

Scenario: you need to upgrade the production inference engine to a newer major version, without changing the model or the weights. How do you qualify the upgrade? <!--anki:6330666162343239616636303438343238383839313961666165643634316438-->
?
1. **Freeze the input**: compare the token IDs rendered by both versions on the parity set.
2. **Compare the effective configuration**: default sampling, logprobs mode, parsers, structured output backend.
3. **Measure parity** with teacher forcing, against the noise floor of the old version.
4. **Replay in generation**: prefix caching, tools, streaming, structured outputs, long contexts.
5. **Test under load** at the target concurrency, preemption included.
6. **Run paired evals**, then a canary before the switch ([[93-monitoring-inference|pre-traffic checks]]).

**Trap**: concluding parity because a few short prompts give the same text.

---

Scenario: after migrating from Transformers to vLLM, your internal eval loses 4 points while the smoke tests pass and the weights are identical. How do you find the cause? <!--anki:3966386132326266363531613466613662323138643033386138663131326439-->
?
1. **Input tokens**: compare the rendered IDs, look for a duplicate BOS or a different template.
2. **Parameters**: spot the default values coming from `generation_config.json`, then set everything.
3. **Teacher forcing**: locate the positions where logprobs deviate beyond the noise floor.
4. **Outputs**: check the tool and reasoning parsers, and the field read by the client.
5. **Examples that change status**: classify them by cause rather than looking at the average.

**Trap**: blaming quantization or the kernels before comparing the input tokens.

---

Scenario: your assistant sometimes produces incoherent text, only at peak hours, and no unit test reproduces it. What do you do? <!--anki:3662626466636331383362303432656639616666626135366531356462373333-->
?
1. **Correlate** the incidents with load, preemption and the prefix cache hit rate in the engine metrics ([[93-monitoring-inference|monitoring]]).
2. **Reproduce** by replaying traffic at peak concurrency, with the parity set interleaved.
3. **Force preemption** by reducing the KV cache memory.
4. **Isolate** by disabling one option at a time: prefix caching, CUDA graphs, speculative decoding.
5. **Roll back** to the last healthy configuration during the investigation, then add the case to the parity set.

**Trap**: attributing the incoherence to the model because the text looks like a hallucination.

---

Scenario: you migrate the embedding encoder of your RAG from a Sentence Transformers script to a vLLM server, with the same weights. Recall@10 drops by 9 points. How do you find the cause? <!--anki:3365613831313437633036623462386661313534353763643538333633333537-->
?
1. **Compare the vectors** of both stacks on the same texts, against the floor measured between two runs of the reference.
2. **Read the effective configuration**: pooling type, normalization, query and document prefixes, maximum length.
3. **Check the Sentence Transformers metadata** of the repository; if it is missing or wrong, set the pooling with `--pooler-config`.
4. **Segment the gap**: truncated texts, languages, short queries.
5. **Reindex** only once parity is restored, keeping the old encoder for rollback ([[22-advanced-rag|advanced RAG]]).

**Trap**: re-encoding the whole corpus with the new server. Queries and documents become consistent again, recall partly recovers, and the wrong pooling stays in place.
