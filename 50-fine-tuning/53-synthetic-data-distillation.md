What is synthetic data in AI Engineering? <!--anki:4a7e713448475b3c212c-->
?
Data **generated by a LLM** (questions, answers, conversations, test cases, preferences) rather than collected or written by humans. Uses: **fine-tuning**, **distillation**, **eval games** ([[94-evals-methodology|golden dataset]]), **adversarial tests**, training of **models of embedding**.

For example, generating rare ticket variants expands a training set, provided you check labels and diversity. The generator can reproduce the same formulations or invent impossible cases. Keep the generation provenance and recipe, and then measure the effect on an independent test containing representative examples of real traffic.

---

How to generate various synthetic data? <!--anki:686f5943344f63512c69-->
?
Avoid “generates 100 examples” (repeated outputs). Instead:
- define **dimensions** (persona, intent, difficulty, length, language) and **cross** their values;
- from **real seeds** (documents, prod requests);
- generate in **two times**: first the entries, then the answers;
- vary the models and the temperature.

Example: Crossing three languages, four intentions and two difficulty levels gives 24 cells to cover, not a guarantee of diversity. Deduplicate paraphrases and control rare categories. Never use the final test as a seed of generation.

---

Why is filtering the most important step? <!--anki:415b72513651782a2d72-->
?
A LLM generates **false, trivial, or duplicated examples** with aplomb. Filter by: **automatic validation** (code execution, schema, verifiable response), **quality judge**, **deduplication** (exact and semantic), and **human proofing** of a sample. Better **1,000 good examples than mediocre 50,000**.

Start by defining what a useful example is: correct answer, adapted difficulty and coverage of a real need. Also audit the rejections, because a filter can eliminate the rare cases that we wanted to learn. Then compare the trained model with a comparable budget; neither the volume retained nor the score of the filter alone prove a gain.

---

What is the collapse model? <!--anki:7a373c34476839424d67-->
?
The **degradation** of a model repeatedly trained on **model outputs**: diversity collapses, **distribution tails** (rare cases) disappear and errors increase. Parade: **mix with real data**, retain original human data, filter heavily.

The risk increases when new generations **replace** original data and forget about unlikely events. This is not an inevitable consequence of any synthetic example. Track diversity, errors and coverage of rare cases over generations; a stable average can mask the gradual erasure of important segments.

---

What is distillation? <!--anki:765861753b4b257a6172-->
?
Train a **student model** (small) to replicate the behavior of a **teacher model** (large) Objective: to keep most of the quality **on a targeted perimeter** with a **less expensive and faster** model to serve ([[121-inference-costs|costs]]).

The student can learn from the teacher's answers or probability distributions when they are available. He also learns his mistakes and limitations: generating and filtering examples counts as much as choosing sizes. Measuring quality, latency and cost on the target task; the transfer of all general abilities is not guaranteed.

---

Do not confuse: distillation "on the outputs" and "on the logs"? <!--anki:477836586d2c757d4b34-->
?
- **On outputs**: learn the sequences produced by the teacher, often by SFT; possible via a textual API if its conditions allow it.
- **On logs**: Bring together the teacher/student distributions, for example via a KL divergence.

Example: the selected text only shows "Paris"; the distribution may reveal that "Lyon" remained plausible. This additional information distinguishes an almost certain target from an ambiguous target.

Comparing token distributions directly by token requires aligned vocabularies and controlled temperature. Some log-probabilities exposed by a API are not the complete distribution; different tokenizers require a suitable alignment method.

---

What is the distillation of reasoning? <!--anki:4c2f403b3b7c5d4f2141-->
?
Fine-tune a small model on the **reasoning traces** of a reasoning model (→ reflection problem → verified answer). Models of a few billion parameters thus gain strongly in maths and code; it is often **more effective than doing RL** directly on a small model ([[138-reasoning-models|Reasoning]]).

The gain depends on the tasks and protocol. Filtering solutions by an independent checker and testing unseen problems. A correct final answer can hide false steps; learning a longer trace does not guarantee better reasoning.

---

What are the legal constraints on distillation? <!--anki:446e5a335b782b4c6679-->
?
The **conditions of use** of many providers prohibit the use of their outputs to **train a competing model**. Check the **license** of the teacher (including open weights models, some of whose licenses impose conditions on derived models) and trace the **origin** of the data ([[155-ai-act|AI Act]], [[105-agentic-ai-devsecops|AI-BOM]]).

Distinguish between the right to use the model, the right to exploit its outputs and the rights to the reproduced content. Check the conditions applicable **to the service, the contract and the date of generation**, then keep this version with the dataset. Public or free access does not, in itself, constitute a commercial retraining authorization.

How to generate synthetic data for evals without skewing the result? <!--anki:637960387b2f456f246f-->
?
- Use an **Different Model** of the one being evaluated.
- Make **reread** the cases by an expert, and keep a share of **real data**.
- Monitor the **Lexical Recovery** (Questions too close to the documents, too easy).
- Never **re-train** on the eval game itself (contamination).

A different generator can share the same biases or have seen the same data: this separation is not enough. Set the skills to test before generation, check the references and reserve the final game. Examine the performance by difficulty and by source to find a benchmark artificially close to the style of the generator.

---

Why deduplicate synthetic data after generation rather than just before? <!--anki:3965633638376131383162613463373762363931623738643464613035613864-->
?
Separate **prompts** can produce almost identical responses, and a generation can copy an example already present in the test, so seed diversity does not guarantee that of the final data.

Deduplicate and group the examples by origin, template and proximity before forming the partitions. Also control the overlap with the protected evaluation. Keep the origin of the generator and filtering to find a defective batch. Measure the diversity of situations and errors covered, not just the number of rows produced.

---

Why balance synthetic data by error type rather than just by class? <!--anki:6331363364303734633131353463313361346162663864353132313762313137-->
?
A class can contain **several difficulties**: negation, ambiguity, OCR noise or incomplete context. A label-balanced dataset can remain almost entirely composed of easy cases.

Build a coverage grid, generate and then verify each family, and compare to actual errors. Do not set the final test on this synthetic grid alone. Artificially difficult but unrealistic examples can divert learning from useful distribution.

---

Calculation: 20,000 synthetic examples cost 0.002 € each; 40% pass filter, then 25% survivors are duplicates. What generation cost e.g. single retained? <!--anki:3730623134303736656363643439343638326535646536633164333036353366-->
?
The generation costs **40 €**. There is **20,000 × 0.40 × 0.75 = 6,000 examples**, or about **0.0067 € example retained**.

Filtering and human validation are not included. Performance must be tracked by category: keeping lots of easy paraphrases can give good overall performance without improving important cases. Deduplicate before distributing training and testing.

## Scenarios

Scenario: your document classification costs 8,000 € per month with a large model. How do you conduct a distillation project? <!--anki:6e3b37725e2a30697767-->
?
1. **Fix the terminals**: reference evaluation with the teacher (ceiling) and with the small untrained model (floor)
2. **Collect real entries** from logs, not invented examples, and have them processed by the teacher
3. **Filtrer**: automatic validation, judge, deduplication, proofreading of a sample. A thousand good examples are better than fifty thousand mediocre
4. **Train the student** in LoRA, then compare on the same eval
5. **Deploy Carefully**: Router with teacher falling back on low-trust cases ([[82-routing-llm|routing]])

**Trap**: Check the teacher’s terms of use too late, while many prohibit training a competitor model.

---

Scenario: to save time, a colleague offers to generate 50,000 synthetic examples with the same model as the one being evaluated. What risks do you report? <!--anki:79712c3b6f2557634d6a-->
?
1. **Contamination**: Evaluate a model on data it produced artificially inflates scores
2. **Model collapse**: looping on model outputs reduces diversity and clears rare cases
3. **Lack of variety**: "generates 50,000 examples" produces repetitive outputs. Cross dimensions and from real seeds
4. **Good practices** : generate with an **another model**, Reread by an expert. Keeping a piece of real data
5. **Monitoring**: lexical overlap, semantic duplicates, real case difficulty

**Trap**: judge the quality of a synthetic game by its volume.
